Measurement Uncertainty Analysis: 1000 µL (1 mL) Micropipette

1. Can the uncertainty be calculated from the attached report?

Yes, partially. The report explicitly provides the Expanded Uncertainty (U) for each nominal volume (e.g., ± 0.52 µL for the 1000 µL / 1 mL setting).

However, the report does not provide a full "Uncertainty Budget" (the breakdown of individual Type B components like balance resolution, water density, temperature variations, and evaporation). We can, however, reverse-engineer the calculation to verify the lab's math and derive the hidden Type B uncertainty using the raw data provided (Mean, Standard Deviation, and Expanded Uncertainty).

2. Detailed Calculation & Verification (Focusing on 1000 µL / 1 mL)

Here is the data extracted for your 1 mL (1000 µL) test:

Parameter Value
Nominal Volume 1000 µL
Mean Measured Volume 997.3963 µL
Systematic Error (Bias) -2.6037 µL
Repeatability (Random Error / Standard Deviation, s) 0.7364 µL
Reported Expanded Uncertainty (U) ± 0.52 µL

Step A: Determine the Coverage Factor (k)

In calibration certificates, the standard convention is to use a coverage factor of k = 2 (representing approximately a 95% confidence level).

Step B: Calculate the Combined Standard Uncertainty (uc)

The Expanded Uncertainty is derived from the Combined Standard Uncertainty:

U = k × uc
0.52 = 2 × uc
uc = 0.26 µL

Step C: Calculate the Type A Uncertainty (Repeatability)

Looking at the data, the lab likely used the Standard Deviation of the Mean (s / √n) rather than the standard deviation of a single measurement. Since there are n = 10 measurements:

uA = s / √n = 0.7364 / √10 = 0.7364 / 3.1623 = 0.2328 µL

Step D: Reverse-Engineer the Type B Uncertainty

Type B uncertainty comes from the calibration setup (balance, temperature, evaporation, etc.). We can find it using the root-sum-square formula:

uc = √(uA2 + uB2)
0.26 = √(0.23282 + uB2)
0.0676 = 0.0542 + uB2
uB2 = 0.0134
uB = 0.1157 µL

(This means the lab's environmental/equipment uncertainty contributes about 0.1157 µL).

3. Crucial Expert Insight: The "True" Uncertainty for your Lab

The Expanded Uncertainty of ± 0.52 µL reported by the lab only represents the uncertainty of the calibration process itself. It does not include the systematic error (bias) of the pipette.

If you use this pipette without applying a correction factor, the actual volume dispensed will be off by approximately -2.6037 µL.

To calculate the Total Measurement Uncertainty for your laboratory's actual pipetting, you must combine the calibration uncertainty with the bias:

1. Convert Systematic Error to Standard Uncertainty (Type B)

Assuming a rectangular distribution for the bias:

ubias = |-2.6037| / √3 = 1.503 µL

2. Calculate Total Combined Standard Uncertainty (utotal)

utotal = √(uc2 + ubias2)
utotal = √(0.262 + 1.5032)
utotal = √(0.0676 + 2.259)
utotal = √2.3266 = 1.525 µL

3. Calculate Total Expanded Uncertainty (Utotal)

Utotal = k × utotal = 2 × 1.525 = ± 3.05 µL

Summary Recommendation

  • Can you calculate it? Yes. The calibration uncertainty is ± 0.52 µL.
  • Should you use ± 0.52 µL in your SOPs/Uncertainty Budgets? No. You must include the bias. Your true expanded uncertainty for a single dispense at 1000 µL is approximately ± 3.05 µL (assuming you do not calibrate the pipette to zero out the -2.60 µL error).
  • Missing Info: A proper ISO 17025 compliant certificate would include an "Uncertainty Budget" table showing exactly how the 0.1157 µL (Type B) was derived (Balance resolution, water temp, etc.). You may want to request this from the calibration vendor if you need to present a full budget to an auditor.
