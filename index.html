1. Purpose

This report establishes, quantifies and documents the measurement uncertainty associated with the bacterial endotoxin result obtained by the kinetic chromogenic method, in accordance with ISO/IEC 17025:2017 clause 7.6. It provides a transparent uncertainty budget that identifies the dominant contributions to the reported endotoxin concentration, and defines the expanded uncertainty to be quoted with the test result.

The report documents the traceability of the measurement to the reference materials used: the Control Standard Endotoxin (CSE) E50-643, Batch 0001365773 and the Kinetic-QCL LAL reagent K50-643, Batch BL036EE6GR, both supplied by Lonza Walkersville Inc. The Certificate of Analysis (CoA) for each material is held on file and is summarised in Sections 3.4 and 3.5.

The report further documents the treatment of the two ELx808 microplate readers that are both in routine use for BET testing. Because either unit may be used for a given test, the reader contribution is evaluated as a combined estimate over both instruments, comprising the pooled within-reader uncertainty and an additional between-reader variance component (Section 6.2).

The detailed derivations, raw data, certificates and statistical calculations are not reproduced in this report. They are presented in Appendices 1 to 5, which form an integral part of this document and to which this report refers directly.

2. Scope

This evaluation applies to the determination of bacterial endotoxin concentration, C (expressed in EU/mL), in haemodialysis concentrated solution by the kinetic chromogenic Limulus amoebocyte lysate (LAL) method, using either ELx808 microplate reader QC/M/001 14 (A) or QC/M/001 14 (B), the micropipettes listed in Appendix 4, Table 1, and the Lonza CSE/LAL system identified in the Document Control table.

Uncertainty is evaluated for:

  • Sample result — endotoxin concentration of the diluted test sample, including the sample dilution step, the final well volume ratio and the standard curve preparation;
  • Positive Product Control (PPC) result — the spiked well used to demonstrate absence of inhibition or enhancement.

The evaluation covers the routine operational range represented by the standard curve (0.01 EU/mL to 50 EU/mL), with the working point of 0.5 EU/mL used as the representative mid-curve concentration for interpolation uncertainty.

Scope note — dual reader use: The estimated uncertainty in this report applies equally to results generated on reader (A) and on reader (B). It is not a reader-specific uncertainty; it represents the uncertainty of the reported result given that either reader may be used. Reader-specific uncertainties are given in Appendices 1 and 2 for information.

4. Measurement Model

4.1 General relationship

The kinetic chromogenic method relates the onset time t (seconds) to the endotoxin concentration C (EU/mL) through an empirical log–log linear regression obtained from the Initial Qualification Test (Lysate Verification) report. The measurand is the endotoxin concentration predicted from the measured onset time:

log10 t = a + b · log10 C (1)

where b is the slope (−0.226), a is the y-intercept (3.075) and r = −0.999 (Appendix 5, Table 1). The reported result for a test sample is obtained from equation (1) and corrected for the sample dilution factor and the final well volume ratio. The concentrations C used in equation (1) are prepared from the CSE lot identified in Table 3.1 and are therefore traceable to the Lonza RSE as described in Section 3.4.

4.2 Dilution and volume ratio

DF = (Vs + Vd) / Vs   =   (0.183 + 3.500) / 0.183 = 20.13 (2)
R = Vs / (Vs + Vr)   =   100 / (100 + 100) = 0.500 (3)

4.3 PPC concentration

CPPC = Cspike × Vsp / (Vs + Vsp)   =   5 EU/mL × 10 / 110 (4)

The 5 EU/mL spike solution is prepared from the same CSE lot (E50-643) used to prepare the standard curve, so the spike concentration carries the same traceability chain as the calibrants.

4.4 Uncertainty propagation model

For independent (uncorrelated) contributions, the relative combined standard uncertainty of the reported endotoxin result is the root-sum-square of the relative standard uncertainties of the individual components:

uc,rel(C) = √ [ urel,pip² + urel,cal² + urel,analyst² + urel,instr² ] (5)

where:

SymbolContributionAppendix / source
urel,pipMicropipette volumes: sample dilution, final well volume ratio, standard curve preparationAppendix 4
urel,calReagent / lysate calibration — standard uncertainty of interpolation from the regression lineAppendix 5
urel,analystAnalyst — intermediate precision, repeatability, calibration curve fit and biasAppendix 3
urel,instrMicroplate reader — combined over both ELx808 units (pooled within-reader plus between-reader)Appendices 1 & 2; Section 6.2

The expanded uncertainty is then obtained using a coverage factor k = 2:

Urel = k · uc,rel = 2 · uc,rel (6)

6.2 Combination of the two microplate readers

Both ELx808 microplate readers — QC/M/001 14 (A) and QC/M/001 14 (B) — are used for BET testing, and either unit may be used for any given test. The reader contribution therefore cannot be represented by a single instrument; it must represent the population of instruments in routine use. A two-level approach is applied:

  1. Within-reader (pooled) component — the pooled variance of the two reader-specific combined standard uncertainties, which represents the typical uncertainty attributable to whichever reader is used;
  2. Between-reader component — the additional variance arising from the difference in performance between the two units, which represents the effect of not knowing which reader will be used for a given test.

The reader-specific combined standard uncertainties are (Tables 6.1, Appendices 1 and 2):

uc,A = √[(0.046)² + (0.0115)² + (0.0034)²] = 0.0475 (8)
uc,B = √[(0.027)² + (0.04159)² + (0.0034)²] = 0.0497 (9)

Step 1 — pooled within-reader variance (equal weighting, two units):

upooled² = [ (0.0475)² + (0.0497)² ] / 2 = [ 2.2563×10−3 + 2.4701×10−3 ] / 2 = 2.3632×10−3 (10)
upooled = √(2.3632×10−3) = 0.04861 (11)

Step 2 — between-reader variance (mean of the two reader values = 0.04860; deviations ±0.00110; sample standard deviation with n − 1 = 1):

sbetween = √{ [ (−0.00110)² + (+0.00110)² ] / (2 − 1) } = 0.00156 (12)

Step 3 — combined reader contribution:

urel,instr = √( upooled² + sbetween² ) = √( 2.3632×10−3 + 2.42×10−6 ) = √(2.3656×10−3) = 0.0486 (13)
Interpretation: The between-reader component (0.00156) is small compared with the pooled within-reader component (0.04861) — approximately 3 % in variance terms — which confirms that the two readers perform comparably and that reader selection is not a significant source of uncertainty in its own right. The combined value of 0.0486 is used in equation (5) for both the sample and the PPC result.

The budget shows that the uncertainty of the reported endotoxin result is dominated by two components: the reagent/lysate calibration (≈ 50 % of the combined variance) and the analyst component (≈ 38 %). Together these account for approximately 88 % of the combined variance. The microplate reader contributes a further ≈ 12 %. The micropipette contribution is negligible (< 0.05 %) and has no practical influence on the reported uncertainty, even though it is retained in the budget for completeness and traceability.

Within the reader contribution, the pooled within-reader variance dominates (0.04861) and the between-reader variance is negligible (0.00156), confirming that the two ELx808 units perform comparably and that the reported uncertainty does not depend materially on which reader is used.

Consequently, any programme aimed at reducing the reported measurement uncertainty should focus on the calibration/regression performance of the reagent system and on analyst-related precision, rather than on volumetric operations or on reader selection.

9. Reporting of Results

9.1 Statement of uncertainty

The endotoxin concentration shall be reported in the following form:

Creported = Cmeasured ± 0.28 × Cmeasured  EU/mL   (k = 2, ≈ 95 % confidence)

For example, a measured endotoxin concentration of 0.500 EU/mL would be reported as:

C = 0.500 ± 0.141 EU/mL   (k = 2, ≈ 95 % confidence)

9.2 Reporting rules

  • The expanded uncertainty is reported with a coverage factor k = 2, corresponding to a confidence level of approximately 95 % assuming a normal distribution.
  • Uncertainty is reported as a relative expanded uncertainty, since the budget is constructed entirely from relative standard uncertainties.
  • The same relative expanded uncertainty (0.28) applies to results generated on reader (A) and on reader (B); the report need not distinguish which reader was used for uncertainty purposes, although the reader identity shall be recorded in the test record.
  • The expanded uncertainty shall be quoted to at most two significant figures; the reported concentration shall be rounded to the same decimal place.
  • The PPC result carries the same relative expanded uncertainty (0.28) and is used solely to demonstrate the absence of inhibition or enhancement; it does not propagate into the sample endotoxin concentration.
  • The CSE and LAL lots used for the result shall be recorded alongside the reported concentration, so that the traceability chain documented in Section 3.4 and 3.5 can be audited.
  • Where the reported result is compared against a specification limit, the decision rule stated in the laboratory's quality manual applies.

10. Assumptions, Limitations and Verification Notes

10.1 Assumptions

  • All input quantities are treated as mutually independent; no covariance term is included. Any correlation between the analyst and reagent components is considered to be small relative to the reported uncertainty.
  • Both ELx808 microplate readers (QC/M/001 14 (A) and (B)) are within their preventive maintenance and calibration intervals (performed 09 September 2026) and are used interchangeably for BET testing.
  • The two readers are treated as a representative sample of the instrument population in routine use; no further reader is expected to be introduced without re-evaluation (Section 10.4).
  • All micropipettes are within their calibration interval and are used within the volume ranges specified in Appendix 4, Table 1.
  • Worst-case certificate values have been used conservatively for the 1 mL pipettes (MM2, MM12, MM29) and for non-certified volume points (183 µL, 800 µL, 900 µL).
  • The CSE (E50-643, Batch 0001365773) and LAL reagent (K50-643, Batch BL036EE6GR) are within their CoA expiry dates and are used as stated on their CoAs.
  • The CSE potency is accepted as stated on CoA E50-643, in reliance on the FDA-recognised exemption from in-house RSE/CSE comparison.
  • Environmental conditions in the laboratory are controlled and were demonstrated not to contribute significantly.
  • The regression model in equation (1) is valid over the calibration range 0.01 EU/mL to 50 EU/mL.

10.2 Limitations

  • This evaluation is specific to the haemodialysis concentrated solution matrix and to the dilution scheme described in Section 4.2. A different dilution scheme would require re-evaluation of the micropipette component.
  • The evaluation uses representative mid-curve performance data (0.5 EU/mL) and may not fully represent performance at the extreme ends of the calibration range.
  • Bias is evaluated from PPC recovery data across three lots and four analysts; it is treated as a precision component of the mean rather than as a corrected systematic effect.
  • The reagent component is derived exclusively from the Initial Qualification Test (Lysate Verification) report and should be reviewed if the lysate supplier, lot or qualification data change.
  • The reader component represents the two ELx808 units currently in routine use. The between-reader variance estimate (0.00156) is based on only two instruments and carries a high degree of statistical uncertainty; it is therefore treated as a small correction and does not dominate the reader contribution. If a third reader is introduced, the between-reader estimate should be recalculated with the larger instrument population.
  • Both CoAs are electronic records generated from Lonza's Part 11-compliant system and are signed by the Lonza QA representative; printed copies are uncontrolled and the current electronic record held on file prevails.
