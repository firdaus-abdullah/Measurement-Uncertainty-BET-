Detailed Calculation of Instrument-Related Measurement Uncertainty

Based on the provided reports, we can perform a detailed, step-by-step calculation of the instrument-related measurement uncertainty for the ELx808 reader. (Note: The slope value has been corrected to 1.672 based on the report).

As established, this does not represent the total uncertainty of the Kinetic Chromogenic BET assay (which requires reagent, standard, and pipetting data), but it provides the exact mathematical derivation for the components present in your documents.

Here is the detailed calculation using the GUM (Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement) bottom-up approach.

Step 1: Define the Model and Identify Sources

For the instrument, the measured Optical Density (OD) is the primary output. The relative combined standard uncertainty \( u_c(OD)/OD \) is the root-sum-square of the relative uncertainties of the available components:

\[ \frac{u_c(OD)}{OD} = \sqrt{ \left(\frac{u_{rep}}{OD}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{u_{lin}}{OD}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{u_{bias}}{OD}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{u_{temp}}{OD}\right)^2 } \]

Step 2: Calculate Individual Components

2.1 Repeatability / Precision (\( u_{rep} \))

  • Source: Absorbance Uniformity Test (Image 2).
  • Data: Overall Plate CV = 4.6%.
  • Calculation: The standard uncertainty of repeatability is taken directly from the overall coefficient of variation.
\[ \frac{u_{rep}}{OD} = 4.6\% = 0.046 \]

2.2 Photometric Linearity / Calibration (\( u_{lin} \))

  • Source: Validation of Optical Measurement (Image 1).
  • Data: Reported Slope = 1.672, Intercept = -0.005, \( R^2 = 1.000 \). The data points (Mean OD vs Concentration) are:
    \( x \) (Conc): 0, 0.0625, 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0
    \( y \) (Mean OD): 0.023, 0.101, 0.199, 0.415, 0.839, 1.654

Calculation: We must calculate the standard error of the regression (\( S_{y.x} \)) based on the reported equation \( y = 1.672x - 0.005 \).

Predicted values (\( \hat{y} \)):
  • \( x=0 \): \( 1.672(0) - 0.005 = -0.005 \)
  • \( x=0.0625 \): \( 1.672(0.0625) - 0.005 = 0.0995 \)
  • \( x=0.125 \): \( 1.672(0.125) - 0.005 = 0.204 \)
  • \( x=0.25 \): \( 1.672(0.25) - 0.005 = 0.413 \)
  • \( x=0.5 \): \( 1.672(0.5) - 0.005 = 0.831 \)
  • \( x=1.0 \): \( 1.672(1.0) - 0.005 = 1.667 \)
Residuals (\( y - \hat{y} \)) and Squared Residuals:
  • \( 0.023 - (-0.005) = 0.028 \rightarrow 0.000784 \)
  • \( 0.101 - 0.0995 = 0.0015 \rightarrow 0.00000225 \)
  • \( 0.199 - 0.204 = -0.005 \rightarrow 0.000025 \)
  • \( 0.415 - 0.413 = 0.002 \rightarrow 0.000004 \)
  • \( 0.839 - 0.831 = 0.008 \rightarrow 0.000064 \)
  • \( 1.654 - 1.667 = -0.013 \rightarrow 0.000169 \)

Sum of Squared Residuals (\( SS_{res} \)): \( 0.00104825 \)

Standard Error of Regression (\( S_{y.x} \)):
\[ S_{y.x} = \sqrt{\frac{SS_{res}}{n-2}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.00104825}{6-2}} = \sqrt{0.000262} = 0.01619 \]
Uncertainty of the Slope (\( u_{slope} \)):
  • Mean of \( x \) (\( \bar{x} \)) = \( 1.9375 / 6 = 0.3229 \)
  • \( S_{xx} = \sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2 = 0.7064 \)
\[ u_{slope} = \frac{S_{y.x}}{\sqrt{S_{xx}}} = \frac{0.01619}{\sqrt{0.7064}} = \frac{0.01619}{0.8405} = 0.01926 \]
Relative Uncertainty of Linearity:
\[ \frac{u_{lin}}{OD} = \frac{u_{slope}}{\text{Slope}} = \frac{0.01926}{1.672} = 0.0115 \text{ (or } 1.15\% \text{)} \]
This 1.15% linearity uncertainty is perfectly in line with expectations for a high-quality microplate reader with an \( R^2 = 1.000 \). The previous misread slope of 1.872 caused artificially high residuals.

2.3 Intercept Bias (\( u_{bias} \))

  • Source: Validation of Optical Measurement (Image 1).
  • Data: Y-intercept = -0.005.
  • Calculation: Treat the intercept as a systematic bias. Assuming a rectangular distribution (since no confidence interval is given):
\[ u_{bias} = \frac{|-0.005|}{\sqrt{3}} = 0.00289 \text{ OD units} \]

To find the relative uncertainty, evaluate it at a typical working concentration, e.g., 0.5 EU/mL (Mean OD = 0.839):

\[ \frac{u_{bias}}{OD} = \frac{0.00289}{0.839} = 0.0034 \text{ (or } 0.34\% \text{)} \]

2.4 Temperature Stability (\( u_{temp} \))

  • Source: Absorbance Uniformity Test - Temperature Log (Image 2).
  • Data: Reads 1-11 are 36.90°C; Read 8 is 37.00°C. Max deviation = 0.10°C.
  • Calculation: Assume a rectangular distribution for the temperature variation:
\[ u_T = \frac{0.10}{\sqrt{3}} = 0.0577^\circ\text{C} \]

Assumption: The LAL kinetic reaction rate changes by approximately 10% per 1°C (a standard conservative biological assumption).

\[ \frac{u_{temp}}{OD} = 0.0577 \times 10\% = 0.0058 \text{ (or } 0.58\% \text{)} \]

Step 3: Combine and Expand the Uncertainties

Now, combine the relative standard uncertainties in quadrature:

\[ \frac{u_c(OD)}{OD} = \sqrt{ (0.046)^2 + (0.0115)^2 + (0.0034)^2 + (0.0058)^2 } \] \[ \frac{u_c(OD)}{OD} = \sqrt{ 0.002116 + 0.000132 + 0.000012 + 0.000034 } \] \[ \frac{u_c(OD)}{OD} = \sqrt{ 0.002294 } = 0.0479 \text{ (or } 4.79\% \text{)} \]

To express the Expanded Uncertainty (\( U \)) at a 95% confidence level, multiply by the coverage factor \( k = 2 \):

\[ U = 4.79\% \times 2 = 9.58\% \]

💡 Expert Note for ISO 17025 Accreditation:
This 9.58% expanded uncertainty represents only the instrument portion of the measurement uncertainty. An assessor will still require you to calculate the uncertainty contributions from the calibration curve, standard endotoxin purity, LAL reagent variability, and pipetting errors. The 4.6% repeatability is the dominant factor here, which is typical for microplate-based assays.
