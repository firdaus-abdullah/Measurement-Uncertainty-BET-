1. Can the uncertainty be calculated from a standard BET report?

Usually, No. A standard laboratory test report or a kit manufacturer's Certificate of Analysis (CoA) typically only provides the final calculated Endotoxin result (e.g., < 0.05 EU/mL) and the passing criteria. It does not provide the statistical raw data required for an ISO 17025 uncertainty budget.

To calculate uncertainty, you must build your own budget using the raw assay data (onset times, regression parameters) and the uncertainty values stated on the CoAs for the Lysate, Control Standard Endotoxin (CSE), and your calibrated pipettes.

2. The Measurement Model for Kinetic Chromogenic

In the Kinetic Chromogenic Assay, the instrument measures the time (onset time, \(T\)) it takes for a specific yellow color to develop. The endotoxin concentration (\(C\)) is calculated using a linear regression of the log-log standard curve:

log(C) = m × log(T) + b
Therefore: C = 10(m × log(T) + b)

Where: m = slope, b = y-intercept, T = onset time.

3. Detailed Uncertainty Budget Components

To comply with ISO 17025, you must combine Type A (statistical) and Type B (systematic) uncertainties using the root-sum-square method:

uc = √(urep2 + uCSE2 + ulysate2 + upipette2 + ucurve2 + uinstrument2)

Component 1: Repeatability (Type A)

Calculate the standard deviation of your sample replicates (typically duplicate or triplicate wells).

urep = Standard Deviation of Sample Replicates / √n (number of replicates)

Component 2: CSE Concentration (Type B)

Look at the Certificate of Analysis for your Control Standard Endotoxin. It will state a concentration (e.g., 10 EU/vial) and an expanded uncertainty or tolerance (e.g., ± 5%). Convert this to a standard uncertainty.

uCSE = (Percentage Uncertainty / 100) × C / √3 (Assuming rectangular distribution)

Component 3: Lysate Sensitivity / λ (Type B)

Look at the CoA for your LAL Lysate. It states the labeled sensitivity (e.g., 0.05 EU/mL) and an acceptable range (e.g., 0.025 - 0.10 EU/mL). The uncertainty is derived from how far the actual sensitivity deviates from the label, or the stated tolerance on the CoA.

Component 4: Volumetric Dispensing (Type B)

This is where your previous micropipette certificates come in! You must include the uncertainty of the pipettes used to dispense the sample, the lysate, and the CSE. Using the data from your previous prompt, if you dispense 100 µL, use the \(U_{total}\) calculated for 100 µL. (Assuming \(k=2\), \(u_{pipette} = U_{total}/2\)).

Component 5: Standard Curve Regression Uncertainty (Type B)

This is often the largest source of uncertainty in KCA. It is the standard error of the regression (residual standard deviation) calculated from the standard curve points.

ucurve = Standard Error of the Estimate (Syx) from the log-log regression

Component 6: Instrument / Incubator (Type B)

Includes the temperature stability of the microplate reader (e.g., 37°C ± 1°C) and the photometric accuracy of the absorbance reader. Check the instrument's calibration certificate.

4. Example Calculation (Hypothetical)

Assume you are testing a sample and the calculated endotoxin result is 0.100 EU/mL.

Uncertainty Component Source / Value Standard Uncertainty (u)
Repeatability (Type A) SD of 3 wells = 0.005 EU/mL 0.005 / √3 = 0.00289 EU/mL
CSE CoA (Type B) ± 5% tolerance (Rectangular) 0.100 × (5/100) / √3 = 0.00289 EU/mL
Lysate CoA (Type B) ± 10% tolerance on λ 0.100 × (10/100) / √3 = 0.00577 EU/mL
Pipette (Type B) U = ±1.49 µL at 100 µL (k=2) (1.49 / 100) / 2 = 0.00745 (Relative)
0.00745 × 0.100 = 0.00075 EU/mL
Standard Curve (Type B) SE of Regression = 0.012 0.012 EU/mL

Step 1: Calculate Combined Standard Uncertainty (\(u_c\))

uc = √(0.00289² + 0.00289² + 0.00577² + 0.00075² + 0.012²)
uc = √(0.0000083 + 0.0000083 + 0.0000333 + 0.0000005 + 0.000144)
uc = √(0.0001944) = 0.0139 EU/mL

Step 2: Calculate Expanded Uncertainty (\(U\)) [k=2 for ~95% confidence]

U = 2 × 0.0139 = ± 0.0278 EU/mL

5. Expert Recommendations for your SOP

  • Do not use the kit manufacturer's overall uncertainty: ISO 17025 requires your lab's specific uncertainty, which includes your analysts, your pipettes, your incubator, and your specific standard curve on the day of the test.
  • Control the Standard Curve: The regression uncertainty (\(u_{curve}\)) usually dominates the budget. Ensure your standard curve has an \(R^2 > 0.980\) and covers the relevant range (e.g., 0.05 to 1.0 EU/mL).
