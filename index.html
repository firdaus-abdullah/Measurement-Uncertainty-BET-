2. Calculation of Components

2.1 Calibration Curve Uncertainty

Using linear regression on log-log data (Slope = -0.236, r = -0.999, n=5):

\( u_{slope} \approx 0.236 \times \sqrt{\frac{1 - 0.999^2}{5-2}} \approx 0.0036 \)
\( \frac{u_{cal}}{t} = \frac{0.0036}{0.236} = 1.5\% \)

2.2 Precision (Repeatability)

Use CV at 0.5 EU/mL (relevant for sample near 1 EU/mL):

\( \frac{u_{rep}}{t} = 2.56\% = 0.0256 \)

2.3 Bias (PPC Recovery)

Mean recovery = 117.7%, SD = 12.1%, n=3:

\( u_{bias} = \frac{12.1}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 7.0\% \)

2.4 Temperature

Max deviation = 0.1°C, assumed coefficient 10%/°C:

\( u_{temp}/t = \frac{0.1}{\sqrt{3}} \times 10\% \approx 0.58\% \)

Note: This budget is incomplete. It does not include pipetting uncertainty, reagent lot variability, intermediate precision (different analysts/days), or standard endotoxin purity. A full ISO 17025 uncertainty budget requires these additional components. The ScienceDirect study on kinetic-colorimetric LAL identifies pipetting and detector noise as the two major uncertainty contributors [reference:2].

4. Missing Components for Full ISO 17025 Compliance

  • Pipetting Uncertainty (1–3%) – obtain via gravimetric calibration.
  • Reagent Lot Variability (3–8%) – lot-to-lot reproducibility data.
  • Intermediate Precision (5–10%) – multi-analyst, multi-day study.
  • Standard Endotoxin Purity (1–2%) – certificate of analysis.
  • Sample Matrix Effects – spike recovery across dilutions.

With these added, the expanded uncertainty (k=2) typically ranges from 15% to 30% for kinetic chromogenic BET.
