1. Purpose and Scope

This report establishes, quantifies and combines the contributions to the measurement uncertainty of endotoxin results obtained by the Kinetic Chromogenic Bacterial Endotoxins Test performed at Duopharma (M) Sdn Bhd. It documents a complete uncertainty budget consistent with the requirements of ISO/IEC 17025:2017 clause 7.6 (Evaluation of measurement uncertainty) and clause 7.8.7 (Reporting of uncertainty), and forms the technical basis for the uncertainty statement attached to endotoxin test reports.

The evaluation covers:

  • Instrument contribution of the ELx808 Microplate Reader (repeatability, photometric linearity, intercept bias).
  • Analyst contribution (intermediate precision, repeatability, calibration-curve fit, bias from PPC recovery).
  • Reagent contribution (uncertainty of interpolation from the lysate verification regression model).
  • Micropipette contribution (dilution factor, final-well volume ratio, PPC spike, standard-curve preparation).

The budget is expressed as a relative standard uncertainty, urel, so that it is transferable across the working range of endotoxin concentrations and directly applicable to any reported result, C.

3. Measurand and Measurement Model

3.1 Measurand

The measurand is the endotoxin concentration C (EU/mL) in the sample well, predicted from the measured onset time t (s) using the log–log linear regression model established during the Initial Qualification Test (Lysate Verification).

3.2 Measurement model

log10 t = a + b · log10 C

with the regression parameters summarised in Table 1.

3.3 BET procedure modelled

The uncertainty budget models the complete analytical sequence for haemodialysis concentrated solution:

  1. Sample dilution: 183 µL sample into 3.500 mL LAL water (DF = 20.13).
  2. Final well: 100 µL sample + 100 µL LAL reagent (volume ratio R).
  3. PPC spike: 10 µL of 5 EU/mL standard into 100 µL sample.
  4. Standard curve: serial dilutions and final 0.2 mL + 0.8 mL dilution.

uc(OD)/OD = √[ (urep/OD)² + (ulin/OD)² + (ubias/OD)² ]

Calculated from the standard-curve slopes of each analyst.

x̄ = (−0.236 − 0.213 − 0.229 − 0.241) / 4 = −0.22975
Sxx = Σ(xi − x̄)² = 0.00044674

uf,i = s / |x̄| = 0.012203 / 0.22975 = 0.0531

6.2 Repeatability, urep

Calculated from the standard-curve CV at 0.5 EU/mL for each analyst.

urep = (2.56 + 0.70 + 1.07 + 1.51) / 4 = 1.46 % = 0.0146

6.3 Calibration curve fit, ucal

Based on the worst-case correlation coefficient (r = −0.996, n = 5 points).

ucal = √[ (1 − r²) / (n − 2) ] = √[ (1 − 0.992016) / 3 ] = 0.05159

6.4 Bias from PPC recovery, ubias

Calculated from the twelve PPC recovery results across all analysts and sample lots.

ubias = s / √n = 15.04 / √12 = 4.34 % → 0.0434 (relative)

9. Combined Standard Uncertainty

The four component uncertainties are independent and are therefore combined by root-sum-square of the relative standard uncertainties:

uc,rel = √[ urel,instrument² + urel,analyst² + urel,reagent² + urel,micropipette² ]

9.1 Sample result

uc,rel = √[(0.0497)² + (0.0872)² + (0.0999)² + (0.00195)²]
uc,rel = √[0.002470 + 0.007604 + 0.009980 + 0.0000038] = √0.020058 = 0.1416

9.2 PPC result

uc,rel = √[(0.0497)² + (0.0872)² + (0.0999)² + (0.00213)²] = 0.1416

12. Reporting the Result

In accordance with ISO/IEC 17025:2017 clause 7.8.7.1, endotoxin results shall be reported together with the expanded uncertainty. The following statement shall be appended to test reports:

Uncertainty statement. The reported endotoxin concentration C is associated with an expanded measurement uncertainty U = 28.3 % of the reported value, at a coverage factor k = 2, corresponding to a level of confidence of approximately 95 %. The uncertainty budget includes contributions from the microplate reader, analyst, reagent (lysate) calibration curve and micropipette operations, evaluated in accordance with ISO/IEC Guide 98-3 (GUM).

13. Observations, Assumptions and Limitations

13.1 Assumptions

  • All component uncertainties are mutually independent (no correlation assumed); combination is therefore by root-sum-square.
  • Certificate expanded uncertainties are normally distributed and were converted to standard uncertainties by dividing by k = 2.
  • The intercept bias of the microplate reader was assumed to follow a rectangular distribution (divisor √3).
  • The uncertainty of interpolation was evaluated at the mid-curve concentration (0.500 EU/mL) with duplicate sample testing (m = 2); this is representative and conservative for the validated range.
  • Worst-case certificate values were used for the 1 mL pipettes (MM2, MM12, MM29), for the 183 µL transfer and for the 800/900 µL transfers, to maintain a conservative estimate.

13.3 Technical observations arising from the source appendices

Traceability note. During compilation of the overall budget, the following typographical/arithmetical issues were identified in the source appendices. None of them alters the final budget values adopted in Table 19, which are consistent with the component summaries of the respective appendices; they are recorded here for traceability and for correction at the next document revision.

  1. Appendix 3, §3.1. The displayed combination formula shows the instrument sub-components, whereas the numerical result (0.0872) correctly corresponds to the four analyst components: √[(0.0531)² + (0.0146)² + (0.0516)² + (0.0434)²] = 0.0872. The formula should be corrected to reflect the analyst components.
  2. Appendix 3, §2.4.5. The standard error of the mean is 15.04/√12 = 4.34 (%), expressed as a relative uncertainty of 0.0434 after normalisation to the mean recovery (102.583 %). The unit should be stated explicitly as relative.
  3. Appendix 4, §5.2. The value 0.072414 is arithmetically incorrect; √[(0.0006)² + (0.001853)²] = 0.001948. The correct value has been used in Table 18 and Table 19.
  4. Appendix 4, §4.1.3. urel(Vtot) is printed as 0.0000154; the correct value is 0.568/3683 = 0.000154, which is the value used subsequently in §4.1.4 and in this report.
  5. Appendix 4, §4.4.4. The displayed expression √[3 × (0.00095)²] does not reproduce the stated result; the reported value 0.00175 corresponds to √3 × 0.00101 (three per-dilution uncertainties). The reported value is adopted.
  6. Appendix 2, §2.2.3.1. Minor inconsistencies exist in the printed predicted values (intercept −0.05/−0.005/−0.055) and in the printed intermediate values; the reported SSres = 0.01374 and Sy,x = 0.05862 are internally consistent with the residual column and are adopted.
  7. Appendix 5, §2.3.3. One observed value is printed as 3.703; the value consistent with the reported residual and mean (3.3703) has been used in Table 8.

13.4 Limitations

  • The budget applies to the Kinetic Chromogenic BET for haemodialysis concentrated solution and matrices validated under the same method; extension to other product matrices requires verification.
  • The budget should be re-evaluated whenever there is a change in microplate reader, analyst cohort, lysate lot, pipette calibration status, or the lysate verification regression model.
  • The reagent component is derived from a single Initial Qualification Test dataset; periodic re-verification (or a pooled estimate over successive verification cycles) will improve the robustness of this component.
  • The evaluation assumes the test is performed under the conditions of the validated method; out-of-specification PPC recovery invalidates the associated result independently of the stated uncertainty.

14. Conclusion

A complete measurement uncertainty budget has been established for the Kinetic Chromogenic Bacterial Endotoxins Test in accordance with ISO/IEC 17025:2017 and the GUM. Four independent components were quantified: instrument (0.0497), analyst (0.0872), reagent (0.0999) and micropipette (0.00195 for the sample result; 0.00213 for the PPC result), expressed as relative standard uncertainties.

The combined relative standard uncertainty is 0.1416, giving an expanded relative uncertainty of 28.3 % at k = 2 (approximately 95 % confidence). This value is fit for purpose for endotoxin limit testing, where results are typically compared against a limit concentration that is an order of magnitude below the endotoxin limit (MVD-based dilution), and where the pass/fail decision is additionally supported by the PPC recovery criterion.

The dominant contributors are the reagent interpolation uncertainty and analyst variability; improvement actions should therefore focus on these two areas — for example, increasing the number of replicates in the lysate verification regression, and periodic analyst proficiency monitoring — rather than on pipette metrology, which contributes less than 0.1 % of the variance.
